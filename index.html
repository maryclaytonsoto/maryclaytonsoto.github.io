About

Where fields overlap

I'm a biomedical engineering and mathematics double major at Duke University's Pratt School of Engineering, with a minor in visual arts. I'm drawn to the places these fields overlap — medical device design, patent law, and the intersection of art and science.

Right now, I'm designing surgical education models with Duke's SEAL Lab through Bass Connections, coordinating cardiovascular device research at the Tallahassee Research Institute, teaching Python to first-year engineers as an EGR105 TA, and serving as Vice President of Professional Development for Theta Tau.

Visual art has always been more than a creative outlet for me — it shapes how I observe, question, and solve problems. Whether I'm drawing hands reaching toward bubbles or 3D-printing molds for hydrogel organ models, I'm driven by the same process: observing carefully, asking questions, and turning abstract ideas into tangible forms.

Visual Arts

Art

Throughout my work, hands and bubbles appear again and again. The hands represent curiosity and the desire to interact with ideas beyond immediate understanding; the bubbles reflect the questions that captivate me — mathematically efficient yet visually unpredictable, governed by surface tension, pressure, and light while appearing almost magical. Existing somewhere between order and spontaneity, they embody the intersection of science and creativity that defines both my artistic practice and my engineering work.

Boundless

Grayscale hands reach toward vibrant bubbles — observation meeting the boundless. A piece about duality: the coexistence of logic and imagination, stillness and motion, the rule-bound and the whimsical.
